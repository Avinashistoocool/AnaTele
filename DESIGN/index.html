    Drone telemetry investigation platform¶

    Design baseline: 29 October 2026
    Status: architecture proposal, not an implementation claim. The existing drone_telemetry.py prototype remains a CSV-only offline demonstrator.

    Executive summary¶

    Build an evidence-based flight investigation workbench. Its main job is to reconstruct what the aircraft and its operator reported, distinguish observations from interpretations, rank plausible failure mechanisms, and make every claim traceable to the original records. It must not issue an unqualified crash verdict or certify that an aircraft is safe to fly.

    The proposed stack is Python/FastAPI, isolated parser and analysis workers, PostgreSQL, S3-compatible immutable object storage, Parquet/DuckDB, and a React/TypeScript frontend using MapLibre and Plotly. Start with ArduPilot tlog and DataFlash support, profile/parameter provenance, a synchronized timeline, a small validated diagnostic rule pack, and evidence-linked reports. Defer arbitrary scripts, broad autopilot coverage, predictive maintenance and elaborate 3D.

    The motivating incident illustrates why this distinction matters: a compass switch before a crash-check disarm is a useful association, but not proof that the compass caused the loss of control. In the inspected Copter 4.7.2 source, the reported AngErr is a thrust-vector tracking error, not a compass/yaw-error measurement.[1]

    1. Assumptions and boundaries¶

    1. The described real log has not been supplied for this design review. Its size, message counts, warnings, distances and measurements are treated as user-reported observations, not independently reproduced results.
    2. The first release is post-flight and read-only. There is no MAVLink connection to an aircraft, parameter-write endpoint, motor-control function or automated flight approval.
    3. ArduPilot is the first fully supported ecosystem. PX4 later receives a separate semantic adapter and rule pack, rather than inheriting Copter rules.
    4. Users may upload incomplete, corrupt, malicious or incorrectly labelled files.
    5. Uploaded locations, operator identities, routes and configurations are private unless deliberately shared.
    6. Human-readable firmware strings may identify an official release or a custom build. Semantics are only as trustworthy as the firmware match.
    7. Thresholds, time windows and performance budgets proposed below are design decisions unless explicitly identified as firmware behavior or documentation.
    8. A profile describes an aircraft; a log describes an observation of that aircraft at a particular time. Neither silently overrides the other.

    2. Product vision and users¶

    The central product promise¶

    Turn a flight log into a reproducible incident investigation: what happened, which explanations fit, what contradicts them, and what evidence is missing.

    The interface should answer these questions in order:

    1. Which aircraft, firmware, configuration and source streams are represented?
    2. Which portions of the recording are usable?
    3. What happened before, during and after each flight?
    4. Which observations indicate a problem?
    5. Which failure mechanisms are supported, and with what limitations?
    6. What additional data or safe inspection would discriminate among them?

    Different users¶

    User Primary problem Product value
    Hobbyist or builder Many warnings, little understanding of their relationships Plain-language explanations, parameter context, precise evidence links
    Commercial operator Need incident triage and an operational record Repeatable reports, flight comparison, annotations and accountable review
    Fleet maintainer Need to distinguish a recurring hardware issue from changing flight conditions Per-aircraft histories, configuration-aware trends and maintenance records
    Developer or investigator Need reproducible details, not a dashboard assertion Raw record access, parser versions, full-resolution plots and competing hypotheses

    Use the same evidence model for all four. Adjust presentation depth rather than maintaining an opaque simplified diagnosis for beginners.

    3. Correctly interpreting the motivating incident¶

    Facts to reconstruct versus conclusions to investigate¶

    Reported observation Defensible interpretation Unsupported shortcut to avoid
    PreArm: Radio failsafe on, throttle about 1000, threshold 975 A radio-integrity/pre-arm problem was reported; investigate its actual trigger and timing “1000 is below 975” or “the threshold caused this”
    GPS “unable to configure band” A receiver configuration operation was rejected “GPS had no usable fix”
    Auto mode not armable Arming was rejected in that mode under the active configuration “GPS caused the arming rejection”
    EKF compass selection changed before disarm An estimator event preceded the reported crash-check action “Changing compass caused the crash”
    AngErr=160>30 The crash detector reported a large thrust-vector tracking error “The compass had a 160-degree yaw error”
    Compasses inconsistent after both incidents Compass checks failed afterward “They were necessarily inconsistent before flight”
    Z vibration peak about 66 A high processed vibration value was observed “Vibration caused the crash”, without timing and persistence
    Clipping count 316 A counter or derived count needs sensor-instance and reset context Sum all sampled counter values and call that 316 new events
    Position estimate approached WP2 by 0.4 m A reported position series was close to the candidate waypoint “The aircraft physically reached it to 0.4 m accuracy”

    Critical firmware details¶

    The Copter-4.7.2 tag was verified at commit fb773567896014da007bca58358704d4a0d8516f. This makes a versioned semantic pack possible, but does not establish that the user's aircraft ran that exact binary.

    For that version, the crash check includes armed/not-landed and mode-related gates, filtered gravity-compensated acceleration below 3 m/s², estimated lean above 15 degrees, thrust-vector tracking error above 30 degrees, and a speed gate below 10 m/s when velocity is available. The gate must persist for a counter equivalent to nominally two seconds at the configured loop rate. The printed error is the value at triggering, not necessarily throughout the interval. The source's executable conditions should take precedence over stale comments.[1]

    This is not a physical-impact detector and does not identify a failed component. The estimated attitude itself can be wrong.

    For the same verified release:

    • ARMING_SKIPCHK=128 skips the board-voltage check, not compass checking. Do not decode this using a remembered ARMING_CHECK bit table.
    • BATT_MONITOR=0 disables that battery-monitor instance. Inspect additional instances before saying that all battery monitoring is absent.
    • FS_GCS_ENABLE=0 disables the GCS-heartbeat failsafe. Whether this conflicts with the intended operation depends on the control-link and mission policy.[2]

    A provisional investigation, not a numeric verdict¶

    The reported observations justify investigating three competing mechanisms:

    1. Estimator/magnetic inconsistency: supported by the reported compass selection and inconsistency messages; weakened as a causal conclusion if the affected core was not primary or the disagreement appears only after impact.
    2. Mechanical/thrust-generation or configuration problem: compatible with loss of thrust-vector tracking; needs motor mapping, target-versus-estimated attitude, ESC evidence and inspection. Apparently successful takeoff is context to consider, not a conclusive exclusion.
    3. Vibration-driven estimation degradation: plausible only if excessive vibration/clipping is shown before the loss of control, not just at impact.

    The radio/pre-arm failures should be a separate finding group. They may share underlying electrical issues, but that relationship must be demonstrated. The platform should not assign numerical root-cause probabilities from this description alone.

    4. Recommended system architecture¶

    flowchart TD
    U[Browser / operator] --> API[FastAPI: auth, metadata, upload coordination]
    API --> PG[(PostgreSQL: jobs, profiles, events, findings, audit)]
    API --> UP[Restricted multipart upload session]
    UP --> RAW[(Quarantined object storage)]
    PG --> S[Job scheduler: leases and retries]
    S --> P[Isolated file parser]
    RAW --> P
    P --> N[Normalize identities, clocks, units, parameters]
    N --> PAR[(Immutable Parquet + dataset manifest)]
    N --> PG
    PAR --> A[Analysis worker: DuckDB + typed rules]
    PG --> A
    A --> F[Evidence graph and ranked hypotheses]
    F --> PG
    API --> Q[Bounded series-query workers]
    PAR --> Q
    Q --> API
    API --> UI[React: timeline, map, plots, evidence panel]
    PG --> R[Versioned report renderer]
    PAR --> R
    R --> REP[(PDF / shareable report artifacts)]

    Technology choices¶

    Layer Recommendation Reason
    API FastAPI, Python, typed request/response models Same language as parsers and analysis; explicit contracts
    Job scheduling PostgreSQL job table with leased workers One operational datastore initially; durable retries and audit
    Parsing Pinned pymavlink adapters; pyulog for later ULog support Existing format implementations, wrapped by platform validation
    Raw/derived artifacts S3-compatible versioned storage Immutable source and output references; large files kept out of SQL
    Metadata PostgreSQL Transactions, tenant isolation, structured relationships
    Bulk samples Parquet grouped by dataset and signal family Efficient bounded interval and column reads
    Analysis/query engine DuckDB in separate resource-limited workers SQL over Parquet without introducing a distributed analytics cluster
    Frontend React + TypeScript, MapLibre, Plotly Linked investigation panels, maps and engineering plots
    First deployment Docker Compose, separate API/scheduler/worker services Easier operation than Kubernetes while retaining isolation boundaries

    Heavy parsing is not a FastAPI in-process background task. It belongs in a separate worker with resource limits and a durable job record.[14]

    Use at-least-once execution with idempotent outputs, not an “exactly once” claim. An analysis identity includes input manifest, profile snapshot, semantic pack, rule-set hash, analysis configuration and engine version.

    Job lifecycle¶

    created -> uploading -> upload_verified -> parsing -> normalizing
        -> analysis_queued -> analyzing -> publishing -> complete

    There are explicit failed, cancelled, and partial_dataset outcomes. An interrupted upload is not the same as a fully received but truncated log. Publish a dataset manifest only after its referenced files pass integrity checks; failed work must not appear as a complete analysis.

    5. Data model¶

    Avoid one table called flights containing everything.

    Entity Purpose and important fields
    Tenant / user / membership Ownership, roles, permissions, audit actor
    Drone Stable physical asset ID; not simply MAVLink system ID
    ProfileVersion Frame, propulsion, sensors, firmware, operating policy, effective interval, provenance
    SourceArtifact Hash, original name, size, object version, format, acquisition metadata
    ParseRun / DatasetVersion Parser/dialect hashes, diagnostics, clock mappings, coverage inventory, Parquet manifests
    SourceIdentity System/component IDs, topic instance, candidate role, link evidence, boot epoch
    RecordingSession Capture session that can contain several aircraft, reboots or flights
    ArmedEpisode Armed interval, possibly entirely on the ground
    FlightEpisode Observed/inferred takeoff and landing intervals, confidence, vehicle and clock epoch
    ParameterObservation Name, typed raw/decoded value, instance, source, requested/reported distinction
    ParameterSnapshot Values and completeness evidence at a time; missing indices and conflicts
    MissionTransaction / MissionVersion Direction, participants, mission type, items, acceptance evidence and uncertainty
    Observation Typed measurement, raw source locator, original time, normalized time, units and quality
    Event / EventEpisode Individual transitions/warnings and derived grouped occurrences
    RuleVersion Applicability, requirements, typed logic, thresholds, tests, validation status, content hash
    RuleEvaluation Triggered, not triggered, unassessable, or not applicable; coverage and reasons
    Finding / Hypothesis Claim, severity, support, contradictions, unknowns, ranking and evidence
    AnalysisRun Exact immutable inputs, rule versions, engine image, options and execution status
    Annotation / MaintenanceAction Human interpretation, inspected component, corrective action, eventual outcome
    Report / Share Versioned output, redactions, access scope, expiry and revocation

    6. Parsing and data integrity¶

    Format detection and isolation¶

    Use explicit file-only adapters:

    adapters\ardupilot_tlog
adapters\ardupilot_dataflash
adapters\px4_ulog
adapters\mapped_csv

    Inspect file signatures and record structure, not just the extension. Pymavlink's convenience dispatcher is not a universal detector: it includes network/device transports and other behaviors that an upload API should never expose. Do not pass an arbitrary user string into that dispatcher.[3]

    Run parsers with no network or credentials, read-only staged input, isolated scratch storage, and CPU, memory, process, output-size and wall-time budgets. Reject unsupported incompatibility flags. Keep the original file even when salvaging its usable records.

    Mission Planner tlog¶

    The inspected Mission Planner writer prefixes packet bytes with an eight-byte big-endian Unix-epoch microsecond timestamp. It records both outgoing and incoming traffic, so call this capture/write time rather than assuming it is always a sensor receive time.[4]

    Preserve the prefix bytes. Pymavlink exposes low timestamp bits as a link value, but the inspected Mission Planner writer does not explicitly reserve them. Do not treat that decoded value as proof of multiple physical links for every Mission Planner recording.[3][4]

    MAVLink validation¶

    Classify every frame as validated, truncated, CRC-failed, signature-unverified, signature-failed, unknown-definition, or unsupported-framing as appropriate. CRC validity is not authentication. A present signature is not a verified signature without the necessary validation context.

    Robust parsing can return bad-data records; it does not repair information. Unknown message definitions may be retained by a parser without undergoing the same checks as known messages. Store unknown payloads and offsets so a later dialect version can reinterpret them.[3][5]

    For MAVLink2, distinguish valid trailing-zero payload truncation from an incomplete/corrupted frame. Preserve whether optional fields were actually available before treating a decoded default as a physical measurement.

    Identities and message loss¶

    Use sender system/component IDs, available link provenance, and boot/session identity. Separate source from command target. Identify the flight-controller stream through heartbeat type/autopilot and corroborating messages, not “ID 1 must be the drone.” A single recording can contain relayed duplicates, GCS heartbeats, radio reports and more than one aircraft.[5]

    Sequence numbers wrap at 256. Evaluate continuity only within defensible source/channel/session streams. A gap does not uniquely prove radio loss: filtering, forwarding, reordering and mixed channels also matter. Report a conditional loss estimate with its assumptions rather than a universal “packet loss percentage.”

    STATUSTEXT¶

    Reassemble long MAVLink2 text using sender/session/message ID and chunk sequence. Preserve partial or conflicting assemblies. Reconstruct bytes before decoding UTF-8 so a character split across chunks is not corrupted.[5]

    Store every warning occurrence. A UI episode such as “Radio failsafe ×47 across 4 minutes” is a derived view, not 47 independent confirmations and not proof that a failsafe flag remained continuously asserted for all four minutes.

    DataFlash, ULog and CSV¶

    DataFlash adapters retain embedded format, unit, multiplier and instance definitions. Preserve native boot timestamps even if a parser supplies a derived wall-clock timestamp. PX4 ULog uses its own formats, subscription IDs, instances and dropout records; do not force these into fake MAVLink IDs.[3][6]

    CSV import requires an explicit mapping for timestamps, timezone/domain, identity, units and null values. Never execute user-supplied timestamp expressions. Units inferred from column names should remain suggestions pending confirmation.

    7. Clock model and synchronization¶

    Maintain at least:

    1. Capture clock.
    2. Vehicle boot clock.
    3. GPS/UTC clock when usable.
    4. Derived investigation clock with mapping uncertainty.

    SYSTEM_TIME can provide boot/Unix relationships. Some timestamp fields permit either boot-relative or epoch values; interpretation must follow the message definition, not one global heuristic.[5]

    Segment credible reboot epochs. Distinguish reboot, delayed delivery and 32-bit boot-millisecond wrap. Record inconsistent anchors and clock jumps. Capture minus sensor time is not automatically network latency.

    When combining onboard and telemetry logs, align through multiple shared landmarks and clock anchors, retain residual error, and make an uncertain match visible. Two records separated by five seconds in display time may not have a defensible causal order if the alignment uncertainty is larger.

    Never interpolate across reboot, estimator-origin reset, long data gaps or incompatible frames. Hold discrete states between observations with a visible freshness limit. Interpolated chart points must never masquerade as original evidence.

    8. Profile and parameter audit¶

    Build this early: diagnostic thresholds and applicability depend on it.

    A profile should contain:

    • Frame class/type and motor geometry.
    • Motor/ESC/propeller specifications and channel-function mapping where known.
    • Firmware name/version, git identity, board and build information.
    • Sensor instances, orientations, device IDs and intended estimator sources.
    • Battery chemistry/cell count/capacity and monitor assignments.
    • Intended control links and failsafe policy.
    • Payload and typical operating envelope.
    • Maintenance changes, configuration versions and known limitations.

    Each field records whether it is operator-declared, firmware-reported, inferred or externally verified. “Quadrotor” in a heartbeat does not alone identify an X-frame geometry, and a generic GPS message does not establish a u-blox model.

    Parameter completeness¶

    973 observed names does not prove a full snapshot. Track param_count, index coverage, sender component, request/response context, repeated values and changes within a collection epoch. A snapshot can be complete, partial, internally inconsistent or of unknown completeness.[7]

    Separate a requested PARAM_SET from a reported PARAM_VALUE. Decode the advertised parameter type and supported value encoding; preserve raw bytes. Do not treat an unacknowledged request as an effective configuration change. For restart-required parameters, reported stored value and active runtime value may differ; retain that uncertainty.

    Audits and comparisons¶

    Version parameter metadata by exact firmware where possible. Diff changes in value, type, meaning, availability and completeness. Do not report a missing parameter in a partial snapshot as “reset to default.”

    A known-good preset is a scoped reference, not a universal safety certificate: it needs frame, hardware, firmware, payload/mission assumptions, provenance, validation owner and expiry/review state. Show why a mismatch matters. Never automatically apply a preset or disable arming checks.

    9. Diagnosis engine¶

    Stage A: establish observations and operating context¶

    Before ranking causes, reconstruct:

    • Vehicle/source and boot identity.
    • Armed episodes versus observed/inferred airborne flights.
    • Mode, mission and parameter state.
    • Available channels, rates, gaps and invalid samples.
    • Relevant pre-arm, takeoff, transition, flight, landing and post-event phases.

    An armed vehicle on a bench is not automatically an airborne flight. Use flight-controller heartbeat and landed-state reports where present; otherwise label phase estimates and their uncertainty.[8]

    Stage B: derive events and indicators¶

    Examples: an arming rejection, a compass-selection change, a rise in clipping, sustained tracking error, a voltage sag, a command-output plateau.

    Use full-resolution observations for detection. A plot's downsampled line is not the input to the diagnostic engine.

    Stage C: evaluate hypothesis rules¶

    id: compass-switch-before-crash-check
version: 1
semantic_pack: arducopter-4.7.2
requires:
  events:
    - crash_check_disarm
    - ekf_compass_selection_change
  context:
    - vehicle_identity
    - clock_epoch
    - flight_episode
correlate:
  same_vehicle: true
  same_epoch: true
  before_seconds: 20
support:
  - compass_change_in_active_ekf_core
  - magnetic_innovation_failure_before_tracking_loss
contradictions:
  - first_attitude_failure_precedes_compass_change
  - changed_core_not_primary
unknowns:
  - missing_primary_core_identity
  - missing_magnetic_innovations
output:
  observed_pattern: compass_event_before_crash_check
  hypotheses:
    - magnetic_estimation_problem
    - vibration_related_estimator_degradation
  probability: null

    This is an illustrative DSL, not executed firmware logic. Its 20-second correlation window is a proposed screening window, not a documented causal threshold.

    Rule results have four states: triggered, not_triggered, unassessable, not_applicable. A failed applicability or coverage check is not a healthy result.

    Stage D: rank without pretending to know probabilities¶

    Start with an explainable, expert-reviewed ranking:

    rank(H) =
    configured prior weight
  + capped support from each evidence family
  - explicit contradictory evidence

    Evidence families might be magnetic/estimator, inertial/vibration, propulsion, power, navigation and communications. Source integrity, timestamp uncertainty, phase relevance and sampling coverage govern whether evidence is usable.

    Do not count every repeated STATUSTEXT message, derivative feature and plot summary as independent support. Do not treat two flights on the same hardware and configuration as independent laboratory replications.

    Display four separate concepts:

    • Severity if the mechanism is real.
    • Comparative investigation priority.
    • Evidence completeness/quality.
    • Calibrated probability, only if validated for that task and population.

    Initially use labels such as strong support, mixed support and limited evidence with explanations, not “92% compass failure.” Future probability calibration requires adjudicated outcomes and held-out evaluation.

    Stage E: recommend discriminating evidence and safe actions¶

    For every hypothesis, answer:

    What observation would most clearly distinguish this from its nearest rival?

    Examples are onboard magnetic innovations, measured ESC RPM/current, a complete parameter snapshot, or qualified inspection of mounting and propulsion. Post-crash recommendations should prioritize safe, propellers-removed inspection and review of existing evidence—not another Auto flight or force-arming.

    10. Eight concrete rule designs¶

    The persistence windows below are initial configurable analysis choices. Firmware gates are separately versioned and must not be confused with them.

    Rule 1: repeated pre-arm radio-integrity failure¶

    • Trigger: repeated Radio failsafe on warnings or an explicitly observed failsafe state during a pre-arm episode.
    • Evidence: mapped throttle input, RCMAP_THROTTLE, calibration, reversal, freshness, receiver failsafe indications, RC options, override activity and timeout settings.
    • Logic: distinguish below-threshold input, lost valid updates and receiver/ override-related mechanisms. In the motivating report, 1000 exceeds 975, so those samples do not establish the below-threshold mechanism.
    • Exclusions: GCS-origin RC messages, old retained values, sparse observations that cannot demonstrate fresh receiver updates.
    • Output: an arming blocker with unresolved or supported trigger branches, not a fabricated adjustment to FS_THR_VALUE.

    Copter's channel state can include overrides; streamed RC values are not an independent capture of the receiver wire protocol.[9]

    Rule 2: arming rejected by mode or throttle state¶

    • Trigger: Auto mode not armable or Throttle too high.
    • Evidence: exact firmware semantics, current mode, arming-command method, Auto options, calibrated throttle/control demand and contemporaneous warnings.
    • Logic: assess each rejected attempt separately. Copter 4.7.2 has an Auto allow-arming option and mode-specific conditions; throttle-high checking is different from low-throttle failsafe.
    • Exclusions: an old mission upload or GPS configuration message merely appearing nearby.
    • Output: distinct rejection reasons, not a single “GPS arming failure.”

    The GPS band warning should create a configuration warning whose operational impact remains conditional on actual fix/accuracy and estimator acceptance.[9]

    Rule 3: estimator/compass event before crash-check disarm¶

    • Trigger: correctly classified compass/core/yaw event within a proposed 20-second pre-disarm window.
    • Evidence: exact warning text, active EKF core, compass device mapping, innovations, target/estimated attitude, current and vibration history.
    • Logic: distinguish compass selection, EKF lane switch, yaw-source change and yaw reset. Record onset ordering relative to tracking deterioration.
    • Exclusions: inactive-core-only changes; post-impact disagreement used as proof of pre-impact failure; correlation based on uncertain clocks.
    • Output: supported chronology and competing hypotheses. Mark any causal interpretation as limited when internal EKF evidence is unavailable.

    Compass switching may be an estimator recovery action rather than the initiating fault.[10]

    Rule 4: loss of thrust-vector tracking¶

    • Trigger: controller/estimate divergence sustained for a configurable 0.5–1 second screening interval, or an observed crash-check disarm message.
    • Evidence: target and estimated orientation, body rates, outputs, filtered acceleration, speed and exact detector semantics.
    • Logic: distinguish an implausible target, a state-estimate jump, and failure to follow a plausible target. Reproduce internal crash gates only if the necessary signals and sampling permit it.
    • Exclusions: mismatched coordinate frames, comparing unaligned signals, treating AngErr as full yaw error.
    • Output: a tracking failure; possible control, estimation and thrust causes remain separate.

    For Copter 4.7.2, use the thrust-vector definition and executable crash gates.[1]

    Rule 5: sustained vibration and accelerometer clipping¶

    • Trigger: initial screening above 30 m/s² for 2 seconds, above 60 m/s² for 1 second, or positive clipping increments in airborne pre-event intervals.
    • Evidence: per-instance vibration and clipping, boot boundaries, sample intervals and phase.
    • Logic: calculate positive counter increments within a valid epoch; report increments over known intervals rather than inventing individual clip times.
    • Exclusions: impact/handling-only peaks, counter resets, mixed IMU instances, and summing cumulative samples.
    • Output: pre-event vibration exposure, impact-only exposure or insufficient temporal evidence.

    ArduPilot's documentation gives 30/60 as useful vibration guidance, not universal crash thresholds. Its vibration metric is filtered, not instantaneous acceleration. DataFlash instance fields differ from MAVLink's clipping0/1/2 representation, so the semantic adapter must normalize them.[11]

    Rule 6: abnormal propulsion-command distribution¶

    • Trigger: output imbalance/saturation persisting in a comparable, nominally steady phase; initially use a configurable two-second screening interval.
    • Evidence: actual channel-function mapping, frame geometry, output limits, desired attitude/rates, achieved motion, ESC RPM/current where available.
    • Logic: compare outputs with expected control demands, not just their spread.
    • Exclusions: takeoff transients, aggressive maneuvers, yaw commands, wind, asymmetric payload and different mixer configurations.
    • Output: command-distribution anomaly. Without measured motor response or inspection, do not claim a particular motor/propeller has failed.

    Motor commands are not measured thrust. Wrong geometry/order/direction requires additional corroboration.

    Rule 7: power monitoring gap or voltage anomaly¶

    • Trigger A: no usable propulsion battery or board-supply observations for the operating policy.
    • Trigger B: sustained sag/undervoltage against an appropriate configured threshold with valid current/load context.
    • Evidence: all monitor instances, voltage/current, board supply, resets, battery profile and arming-check configuration.
    • Logic: separate an observability defect from observed power degradation.
    • Exclusions: invalid sentinels, disconnected/unused monitor instances, chemistry/cell-count mismatch and sparse unaligned current measurements.
    • Output: monitoring coverage gap, observed voltage anomaly, or unassessable battery condition. ARMING_SKIPCHK=128 is contextual risk in the verified firmware, not proof of an electrical cause.[2]

    Rule 8: communication/navigation policy mismatch¶

    • Trigger: required RC/GCS/GNSS service becomes unavailable, or configured failsafe behavior contradicts the declared operating policy.
    • Evidence: relevant source freshness, explicit failsafe events, GCS role, RC availability, mode, mission policy, fix/accuracy and estimator use.
    • Logic: distinguish a radio's status report from actual vehicle link health and a GNSS configuration warning from failed positioning.
    • Exclusions: optional GCS loss during a deliberately permitted autonomous mission; message absence when a topic was never streamed.
    • Output: actionable policy mismatch or loss evidence, not “unsafe because FS_GCS_ENABLE is zero.”

    11. Mission reconstruction and analysis¶

    Treat uploads as transactions with uncertain outcomes¶

    Recognize the direction of MISSION_COUNT, requests, items and ACKs. An upload and a download can carry similar messages in opposite directions. Retransmissions are not automatically new plans.[12]

    Maintain separate:

    1. GCS-intended mission versions.
    2. Observed upload/download transactions.
    3. Acceptance evidence.
    4. Inferred/confirmed onboard mission state.
    5. Execution observations.

    A confirmed shorter full upload replaces the earlier plan; do not keep old trailing points. Partial writes need a known baseline. The MAVLink documentation notes non-atomic ArduPilot upload behavior; therefore, do not guarantee that the last accepted mission remains onboard after an interrupted overwrite. Model possible partial modification unless the exact firmware/transaction evidence establishes otherwise.[12]

    Use available opaque plan IDs as corroboration, not universal identifiers. Missing or zero extension fields do not establish plan identity.

    Reached versus observed near¶

    Use explicit statuses:

    reported_complete
geometry_consistent_with_completion
completion_not_observed
not_visited_in_observed_trajectory
unassessable

    Store the command itself. A takeoff item is not an ordinary horizontal waypoint. ArduPilot commonly uses sequence zero for home; never relabel by display order without retaining original sequence numbers.[12]

    A missing MISSION_ITEM_REACHED does not prove failure to reach, and a MISSION_CURRENT advance can reflect a jump or set-current operation. Generic MAVLink waypoint fields do not guarantee that a given Copter release implements each field. In particular, do not invent a universal waypoint yaw acceptance condition.[12][13]

    For the reported 0.4 m approach, show:

    • Candidate mission version and item command/frame.
    • Nearest recorded horizontal distance.
    • Vertical error only when frames/datums are compatible.
    • GPS/estimator uncertainty and observation gap.
    • Whether interpolation was used.
    • Whether an execution completion message was observed.

    Call the 21.6 m value a recorded-position distance to a waypoint unless an along-path definition and correct active plan are established.

    Geometry and mission warnings¶

    Distinguish MSL, ellipsoid, home-relative and terrain-relative altitude. Home-relative altitude is not generally AGL. Preserve local NED/ENU and body FRD semantics; local frames require a known origin. GPS course is not vehicle heading, and the estimated global position is not physical ground truth.[5][13]

    Cross-track distance must name its reference: a straight mission segment is not the controller's curved/spline path. Plan-level battery margin is unassessable without sufficient battery/energy observations and a validated load model; distance alone is not a credible energy estimate.

    12. Interface and reports¶

    Investigation screen¶

    Use one time-selection model shared by:

    • Severity-filtered event timeline.
    • Map and planned/flown path.
    • Linked engineering plots.
    • Parameter changes and flight-phase markers.
    • Hypothesis cards with support/contradictions.
    • Raw record/evidence inspector.

    Clicking a finding selects its interval and opens the exact source records plus bounded before/after context. A warning episode expands to individual messages. Show missing/invalid data as gaps, not zeros.

    Compare flights by elapsed time from takeoff or equivalent mission phase while retaining actual timestamps and durations. Do not automatically equate “WP2” between different mission versions.

    3D¶

    Start with correct 2D and altitude plots. Later add 3D with explicit altitude datum and frame conversion. Interpolate quaternions with sign continuity; unwrap displayed angles and stop interpolation at gaps/resets. A visually smooth trajectory must not imply more accurate measurements.

    Reports¶

    Each report includes:

    1. Scope, aircraft/profile identity, firmware match and data limitations.
    2. Observed incident sequence.
    3. Ranked hypotheses and supporting/contradicting evidence.
    4. Parameter and monitoring concerns.
    5. Missing evidence and recommended safe follow-up.
    6. Analysis/rule versions and immutable evidence links.

    An optional LLM can phrase already-approved structured findings. It must not invent diagnoses, thresholds, source citations or repair instructions. Log strings are untrusted input and must not become model instructions. Provide a deterministic report fallback.

    13. Fleet trends and maintenance¶

    First implement condition trends, not remaining-useful-life claims.

    Compare like-for-like flight phases while accounting for:

    • Firmware/profile revisions and sensor replacement.
    • Payload, battery identity, temperature and environment.
    • Maneuver intensity, duration and recording rates.
    • Sensor instances, units and signal coverage.

    A vibration trend is not automatically motor wear. Motor wear needs appropriate response measurements and maintenance outcomes. Battery degradation needs consistent battery identity and comparable charge/load/temperature conditions. Record maintenance actions, replaced parts, inspection findings and subsequent flights so useful labels can accumulate.

    Do not compare a sparse radio log with a high-rate onboard log as if their peaks and counter visibility were directly equivalent.

    14. Rule extensibility¶

    MVP: a bounded DSL¶

    Allow registered signals, comparisons, durations, aggregates, temporal joins, exclusions and templated evidence. Validate units, expected types, applicable firmware and required coverage at rule creation.

    Every shared rule needs a version/hash, author, tests, description, applicability, known confounders, validation state and review history. A tenant pins a rule version; publishing an update must not silently rewrite old investigations.

    Later: isolated executable extensions¶

    Do not execute user Python in the API or privileged analysis process. If scripts become necessary, use reviewed immutable packages in disposable sandboxes: no network, no secrets, read-only scoped inputs, strict CPU/RAM/process/output budgets, deadlines and validated result schemas. Do not expose a container-engine socket. Hostile public plugins need stronger isolation than ordinary shared containers before launch.

    15. Testing and evaluation¶

    Four test layers¶

    1. Parser fixtures: valid formats, wrong suffixes, truncated frames, missing chunks, unknown messages, CRC/signature states, reboots and corrupted regions.
    2. Semantic fixtures: version-specific parameter bitmasks, message units, instance mappings, time anchors, arming versus airborne state, mission retransmission/partial-write behavior.
    3. Diagnostic fixtures: positive cases, healthy counterexamples, missing channels, contradictory evidence and known confounders.
    4. End-to-end investigations: immutable expected event sequences, evidence locators, findings and report versions.

    The motivating log should become a manually annotated regression case, ideally paired with its onboard logs and inspection outcome. Do not make it the entire evaluation set.

    Ground-truth levels¶

    Distinguish hardware-confirmed causes, expert-adjudicated interpretations and weak labels from operator narratives. Do not train or score “root cause” against an unverified description as if it were ground truth.

    Include healthy flights, recoveries, aborted takeoffs, bench activity and benign warnings—not only crashes. Split by aircraft/operator and time; keep duplicate recordings and related flights out of opposing splits.

    Measure:

    • False alerts per flight-hour and per flight phase.
    • Precision/recall of observable events.
    • Top-k hypothesis recall on adjudicated incidents.
    • Correctness of cited evidence and timeline order.
    • Abstention and coverage when signals are missing.
    • Calibration only for outputs that claim probabilities.
    • Analyst time to identify and verify a useful explanation.

    A high recall model that blames a compass on every crash is not a useful product.

    16. Security, privacy and operational reliability¶

    • Tenant authorization must follow every data/evidence relationship.
    • Treat parser code as exposed to hostile files. Sandbox and fuzz it.
    • Validate actual file sizes and content; bound expansion if archives are later accepted. Do not trust filenames, MIME types or client-reported hashes.
    • Use unique immutable object versions; object-store multipart ETags are not a substitute for the platform's chosen integrity hash.
    • Keep tenant-scoped caches and deduplication semantics to avoid exposing another tenant's upload or analysis.
    • Redact home coordinates, routes, identities and timestamps where required. Redaction must cover PDFs, metadata, downloadable artifacts and maps, not just visible text.
    • Use revocable application-level share tokens with narrow scope and expiry. Issue short-lived object URLs only after authorization; presigned storage URLs are bearer credentials and may be reused until expiry.[15]
    • Define deletion across raw data, Parquet, indexes, caches, reports, shares and backups; state backup-retention limits clearly.
    • Maintain access logs, restore tests and incident procedures.
    • Explain third-party map/LLM processing. Offer a no-external-services workflow for sensitive logs.

    17. Performance and large-log design¶

    Define test workloads rather than inventing performance claims:

    Workload What to measure
    Roughly 55,000-message tlog Time to first useful preview and final analysis
    Ten-million-message onboard dataset Parse throughput, peak RAM and scratch storage
    1 GB interrupted upload Resume behavior, integrity validation and cleanup
    Multiple tenants Queue fairness, cancellation and query latency under load
    Corrupt/high-cardinality data Resource ceilings, bounded recovery and useful error output

    Stream records in bounded batches. Do not retain millions of Python message objects or emit one JSON blob per full flight to the browser. Group Parquet by dataset/signal family with suitable row groups; avoid one tiny file per message type or sensor sample.

    Series-query requests specify allowlisted channels, interval, source, quality policy and point budget. Enforce bytes-scanned, execution-time and output caps. Use min/max envelopes or other spike-preserving chart summaries, with exact evidence windows fetched on demand. Analysis operates on full-resolution data, with sufficient pre/post overlap at chunk boundaries.

    Cache by tenant, dataset/time-mapping version, channels, interval, aggregation and redaction policy. Benchmark on explicitly stated hardware before promising interactive latencies.

    18. Roadmap and MVP acceptance¶

    Milestone 0: establish the evidence contract¶

    • Obtain permitted, representative logs and paired onboard/telemetry recordings.
    • Annotate the motivating case without assuming its cause.
    • Freeze canonical identity, time, provenance and quality semantics.
    • Define firmware-match and data-coverage policies.

    Exit: two analysts can distinguish observations, interpretations and unknowns in the same case without relying on screenshots alone.

    Milestone 1: reliable ArduPilot ingestion¶

    • Tlog and DataFlash adapters, immutable artifacts and job lifecycle.
    • Source-role inventory, clock epochs, corrupt-region diagnostics.
    • Parameters/profile snapshots and channel coverage.

    Exit: accepted input is reproducible and every normalized record can be traced to its original artifact; partial data cannot appear complete.

    Milestone 2: investigation UI and baseline health¶

    • Armed/airborne episodes, warning grouping, mode/failsafe timeline.
    • 2D planned/flown map, linked plots and exact evidence navigation.
    • Basic mission-transaction history and version-aware parameter audit.
    • Vibration, radio, tracking and monitoring checks.

    Exit: the motivating case can be investigated end to end without a generated root-cause verdict.

    Milestone 3: diagnostic MVP¶

    • A small expert-reviewed rule pack covering the eight patterns above, initially prioritizing radio pre-arm failure, vibration/clipping, tracking loss and compass-event association.
    • Hypothesis ranking with contradictions and abstention.
    • Versioned operator reports and manual annotations.
    • Regression corpus including benign and missing-data cases.

    MVP acceptance: on the motivating case, correctly distinguish the 1000/975 issue, interpret crash AngErr, avoid summing clipping counters, distinguish takeoff from waypoint commands, and avoid treating absent completion telemetry as definitive non-completion. Every finding has evidence and limitations.

    Milestone 4: operational hardening and expansion¶

    • Tenant isolation, access/retention controls, quotas, recoverability and benchmark budgets before accepting broad external uploads.
    • PX4 ULog with its own semantic/rule pack; explicit CSV mapping.
    • Cross-flight comparisons, improved 3D and maintenance records.

    Milestone 5: outcome-driven learning¶

    • Fleet condition trends and validated maintenance outcomes.
    • Optional learned hypothesis ranking or evidence prioritization.
    • Calibrated probabilities only if supported by held-out data.

    If time is short, cut arbitrary scripts, LLM narratives, public rule marketplaces, live telemetry, predictive-maintenance claims and sophisticated 3D first. Do not cut provenance, profile/version awareness, data-quality reporting, counterexample tests or the distinction between unknown and healthy.

    19. Important challenges to the original plan¶

    1. Move profile/parameter handling forward. It is a dependency of trustworthy health and root-cause rules, not a later cosmetic feature.
    2. Treat mission reconstruction as state estimation. Interrupted uploads and missing messages can make the active plan genuinely unknowable.
    3. Do not equate warning order with causality. Recovery actions often occur after a problem has already begun.
    4. A tlog cannot supply information never streamed. Requesting onboard data is sometimes the correct product output.
    5. “100% analyzed” must not mean “all subsystems healthy.” Publish a capability and coverage matrix alongside every report.
    6. Frame and sensor identity are temporal. Components can be replaced; an IMU index is not a permanent physical identity.
    7. A baseline may already be unhealthy. Personalized thresholds need reviewed reference flights, not just the aircraft's historical average.
    8. A smooth 3D replay can be misleading. Keep uncertainty and missing data visible; graphics do not strengthen the underlying measurement.
    9. Root-cause labels are expensive. Maintenance/inspection outcomes and expert review are core product data, not optional future polish.
    10. Models are optional, evidence is not. A System-One classifier may later help prioritize reviewed evidence; it should not replace exact protocol reconstruction or invent causal certainty.
